# Nutrition by Genetics: your theme map

## A map of research themes, not a diagnosis table

These scores roll up Nutrition by Genetics themes so you can see where food-pattern discussion may be worth more attention. They are educational composites. They do not replace labs or a clinician visit.

## What a theme score is (and is not)

A theme score is a research-style summary. It groups related nutrition genetics findings so the page is readable. This card explains why a theme exists. It does not compute meals, grams, or swaps. Called genotypes live on your genetics report.

A band such as Optimal, Moderate, or Needs Attention is a conversation flag. It is not a medical label. A higher or Needs Attention theme means that area may be worth more attention in food-pattern discussion. It does not mean a condition is present. It does not predict disease, drug response, or weight change.

This roll-up does not list rs IDs. Child theme cards name only the confirmed variants from the LifeMetrics Nutrition Genetics extract. If a report header count is higher than the confirmed list, those extra variants are not on this extract.

## How genetics, labs, and gut scores sit on one page

Genetics, blood labs, and gut scores may appear together because they are context layers. Labs are not proof that a gene is working. A lipid panel, a glucose lab, or a gut score, if already on file, helps a clinician more than genes alone. This page does not invent missing lab values and it does not reprint conflicting report ranges.

Do not treat a missing ratio or an empty recommendation line as a clinical finding. If a score does not return, that is a gap in the report, not a number you should act on.

## Why meal amounts live on a different page

Meal amounts and food swaps are calculated on the meal plan, not on this genetics map. Via Cura delivery education, when you use it, stays on bioavailability cards. This genetics map does not import those claims and it does not set a plate.

Child themes with extra safety rules (hunger and fullness, food sensitivities, insulin) are not restated here as certainty. If one of those themes is highlighted, open that card and keep the caution that lives there.

## How to read Needs Attention vs Moderate vs Optimal without panic

Read the bands as a map, not a verdict. Optimal does not mean you can ignore food pattern. Moderate does not mean you are halfway to a diagnosis. Needs Attention does not mean you failed a test.

If a child theme is flagged, open that education card. If you already have labs, bring them to a licensed clinician if you want medical interpretation. Do not change medicines from this page.

## Key takeaways

- A higher or Needs Attention theme means discuss that area. It does not mean a condition is present.
- Child themes with extra safety rules (hunger, food sensitivities, insulin) are not restated here as certainty.
- Meal grams and swaps are calculated separately.
- This page does not list rs IDs. Confirmed variants live on child cards.
- Conflicting report ranges and empty recommendation lines are not clinical orders.

## What to do next

Open the theme cards that are highlighted. Bring existing labs to a licensed clinician if you want medical interpretation. Do not change medicines from this page.
